# Personal outreach

Apply [`core.md`](./core.md) first. This file is the genre overlay for
email, DM, Signal, and text to people you actually know.

Landing pages can say "we." These messages are from one person. Default
to **I**.

---

## Tone

Confident but conversational. Direct without being aggressive. A sharp
friend who happens to know their stuff, not a salesperson, not a
professor.

Contractions. Casual phrasing that you would actually send. Never em
dashes. Practical over aspirational: time saved and problems solved, not
"transforming the future of work."

---

## Principles

**Relationship first.** Lead with warmth and shared history before news,
asks, or context.

**Ask before you broadcast.** A genuine question beats a status update.
Invite dialogue; don't just transmit.

**One primary ask per message.** Stacked asks dilute each other. Pick
the real one and let the rest be context. Across parallel motions
(product invite vs "please share this post"), one ask per person per
week.

**Links as context, not CTAs.** Drop them inline ("the site is at X")
rather than isolated on their own lines, which reads as marketing.

**Easy out.** Make it cheap to ignore. "No stress if the week's packed"
is better than manufactured urgency.

**Thank-you, specific, soon.** One line naming what they actually did.
That is what keeps the favor-budget refilling.

---

## Banned in this genre

- "Hope this finds you well."
- "I'm excited to announce."
- "Journey."
- Feature dumps. One sentence on what it is, then the ask.
- Upvote asks on Hacker News, ever. HN detects rings. If you mention a
  Show HN to a friend, no link and no ask. A substantive comment from
  someone who knows the domain is the only useful favor.
- Generic "let me know if you have any questions."

---

## Shape of a message

1. **Anchor.** Something real: last exchange, their world, a shared
   fact.
2. **Frame.** One or two sentences of what happened, in voice.
3. **Ask.** One thing, concrete, sized to the relationship.
4. **Out.** Permission to skip it.

Rewrite per person. Seeds are not scripts.

---

## Prompt phrasing (for an agent drafting a note)

Write in a confident, conversational tone. First-person "I" voice.
Direct and practical, no corporate jargon, no hype. Short punchy
sentences mixed with longer ones. Uses contractions and casual phrasing.
Never uses em dashes. Focuses on real outcomes (time saved, problems
solved) over abstract vision. Sounds like a smart friend giving honest
advice. One ask. Lead with the relationship.
