People who live with a smell stop noticing it. Generated prose does the same trick: the tics fade into the furniture until someone from outside the room names them.

**aiBreze** is that naming, then the spray. Markdown rules you keep next to the work. Direct, slightly tired, useful. Not insightful-sounding.

[Install](/install) · [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/aibreze)

## What you get

A shared core (register, hard bans, cadence budgets, an applicability heuristic so you do not "correct" real vocabulary), a paste-in smell audit, a claims review that checks substance before cologne, and short genre files for essays, landing pages, outreach, and ship posts.

Genre files are additive. They point at core. They do not copy the bans.

## Install

```bash
pnpm add -D aibreze
```

Copy `node_modules/aibreze/rules/cursor.mdc` into `.cursor/rules/aibreze.mdc`. Point "Full rules" at `node_modules/aibreze/rules/core.md`. Keep a short project overlay for pronouns, carve-outs, and protected lines.

The rules are also importable from Node, and as `aibreze/rules/core.md` from `node_modules`.

## What it is not

A vibe, or a prompt you paste once and forget. Not a second house style that restates the bans in every repo until they diverge.

If a term names a real part of the work, leave it. Ban empty intensifiers and agent theater. Cut theater; keep dialect.
